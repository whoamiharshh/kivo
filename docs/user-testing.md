# User-testing script

Five people, separately, 12–15 minutes each. The goal is to find where someone
hesitates — not to collect compliments.

## Who to ask

Delhi college students who have actually run a shared kitty: a trip, a flat's
groceries, or a club fund. Ask which one they've done and use that word all
session. Someone who has never been the person collecting the money will not
notice the thing this is meant to fix.

## Before they arrive

- The app is open on **their** phone, not yours. This is a mobile product.
- A second pot already exists, **already full**, with a ₹500 limit — so you can
  show the approval rule without building it up from scratch. It has to be
  full: money cannot leave a pot that hasn't reached its target.
- A third pot already exists whose deadline is a few minutes away and whose
  target nobody will reach, so you can show what happens when a pot doesn't
  fill up in time.
- A second Google account ready in another browser profile, to play the friend
  who approves.
- Record nothing without asking. No names in the log.

## Say this, then stop talking

> "You and your friends are putting money together for **\<their own example\>**.
> Set that up here, however you'd do it. Say out loud whatever you're thinking,
> including when something is annoying. I can't help you, and nothing you do
> can break it — if you get stuck, that's the app's fault, not yours."

Then be quiet. Count to ten before answering anything. If they ask "what do I
press?", say "what would you expect to press?"

## Tasks, in order

1. Make a pot for the thing they just described — including how much the group
   needs and by when.
2. Get the invite to a friend the way they normally would.
3. Put their share in.
4. In the pot that is already full, repay a friend ₹300 for something.
5. Repay someone ₹1,000 in the pot with the ₹500 limit. *(Watch what they
   expect to happen here. This is the rule the whole product rests on.)*
6. As the friend, in the other browser, approve it.
7. Close the pot and say who got what back.
8. Open the pot that didn't fill up in time and get their money back out of it.

## Watch for

- At "How much does the group need?" — do they expect the app to charge people
  automatically? ("That's about ₹X each" is only a suggestion.)
- At "By when?" — do they understand that money can't go in after that, and
  that a pot which misses its target gives everyone their money back?
- At "Each person can repay, in total, up to" — do they read it as a cap on the
  whole pot, as a limit per repayment, or as a running total per person? (It is
  a running total per person: once they've used it up, every further repayment
  needs an approval.)
- Does anyone look for a fee, a balance to top up, or a password?
- At step 4, do they expect to be able to repay someone before the pot is full?
- At step 5, do they understand *why* it is waiting, without being told?
- At step 7, can they predict their own refund before it happens?
- At step 8, do they expect their money back automatically, or do they look for
  something to tap?
- Does any word confuse them? Note the exact word they used instead.

## The questions that matter, at the end

1. Whose money is in the pot right now? Who could take it out?
2. If your friend who usually collects everyone's money saw this, what would
   they say?
3. What would stop you using this for the next trip?
4. Was anything here dishonest or confusing about what the money is?

## Afterwards

Write it up in `feedback-log.md` **the same day**, while you still remember the
pauses. One entry per person. Record what happened, not what you hoped would.
If only two people show up, the log says two.
